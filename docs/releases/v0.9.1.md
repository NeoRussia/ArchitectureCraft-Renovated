A lighting release. Shape side faces keep the shading the blocks around them have
under a shader pack, and Pulsar's RGB light engine now sees shapes: the light
falling on them, what they emit, what they let through, and the light of one
held in hand.

Fixes
-----
- **Shape side faces match the blocks beside them under a shader pack.** The
  renderer replaced a shape's vertex colour with white whenever a pack was
  active, while vanilla blocks keep the face shade in their own vertices: 1.0 on
  the top, 0.8 and 0.6 on the sides. A shape's sides therefore read lighter than
  the block next to it while its top matched. Shapes now keep the same shade; an
  emitting shape stays full-bright, so its bloom survives.

Additions
---------
- **Pulsar RGB.** Shape faces take Pulsar's per-position RGB light exactly as the
  blocks around them do, by applying the same per-vertex tint Pulsar applies to a
  block after drawing it. A shape of a lamp or glowing material emits that
  material's colour at the shape's own light level (a non-glowing shape emits
  nothing), light passing through a stained-glass shape is filtered like the
  glass, and a shape held or dropped lights at its level. All of the data comes
  from Pulsar's own palette and filter files, so a material's entry in
  `pulsar-colors.cfg` / `pulsar-filters.cfg` applies to its shapes as well.

Requirements and install
------------------------
- Minecraft 1.12.2, Forge 14.23.4.2747, the version it is built against; the
  14.23.5 line works too. Java 8, and Cleanroom 0.7.x.
- No required dependencies and nothing new to install. Without Pulsar the mod
  renders as before; the Pulsar support is inert. The shape block does not
  reference Pulsar itself: a coremod adds its light interfaces at class load,
  only when Pulsar is present, using the ASM Forge already ships.
- Drop the jar into `mods/` and start the game. Existing worlds keep their shapes
  and their orientation.

Known limitations
-----------------
- A shape held in hand lights in white, not in its material's colour: Pulsar's
  held-item path reads only the block and metadata from the stack, and a shape's
  metadata carries its light level.
- Emission and filtering are per cell, not per face. A shape has two materials,
  so the brighter emission and the stronger absorption are used.
- The Distant Horizons limitation is unchanged: its LODs are keyed on the block
  state, and a shape's material is in the tile entity. Add
  `architecturecraft:shape` to `ignoredRenderBlockCsv` in `DistantHorizons.toml`
  if you would rather have them absent.
